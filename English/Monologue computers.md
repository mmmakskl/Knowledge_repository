**Monologue on Computer Essentials**  

**Introduction**  
<span style="color: green;">Have you ever stopped to think how a simple machine can transform the world?</span> As Bill Gates once said, <span style="color: green;">"The computer was born to solve problems that did not exist before."</span> Today, **computers** are not just tools—they are the backbone of modern life. <span style="color: green;">From **performing calculations** at lightning speed to **storing information** that spans centuries, these devices have reshaped our reality. In this speech, I will explain how computers work, trace their evolution, explore the power of supercomputers, and propose a creative advertising idea for the future.</span>  

**How Do Computers Work?**  
<span style="color: green;">To begin with,</span> every computer relies on two core elements: **hardware** and **software**. **Hardware** includes physical components like the **CPU**, **hard drive**, and **RAM**, which **make up the central processing unit**. **Software**, on the other hand, consists of programs that **receive commands** and **accomplish tasks**, such as editing photos or browsing the web. <span style="color: blue;">When you **turn on a hardware device**,</span> the **motherboard** coordinates communication between parts, while **input** devices like the **keyboard** and **mouse** allow users to **enter commands**. <span style="color: green;">Moreover,</span> **output** devices such as **printers** or **monitors** display results, completing the cycle. <span style="color: blue;">Without this collaboration,</span> computers would be unable to **perform calculations** or **generate output**.  

**History of Computers**  
<span style="color: green;">Moving forward,</span> the journey of computers is a tale of incredible progress. Early machines like **mechanical adding machines** could only **compute numbers** and **take up entire rooms**. <span style="color: blue;">However,</span> the **development of transistors** and **integrated circuits** allowed computers to **evolve from cumbersome devices** into compact, powerful tools. For example, my **PC** today **fits on my desktop**, yet it **has incredible processing power** compared to the **vacuum tube-based systems** of the 1940s. <span style="color: green;">Additionally,</span> pioneers like Charles Babbage **are credited with inventing the computer**, laying the groundwork for modern **software** and **parallel processing**.  

**Supercomputers**  
<span style="color: green;">Now, let’s dive into the giants of computing: supercomputers.</span> Unlike **general-purpose machines**, a **supercomputer** uses **parallel processing instead of serial processing** to **crunch complex problems** like climate modeling or drug discovery. Take the **Supercomputer Center at SPbPU**, for instance. <span style="color: blue;">With a **peak performance of 1.26 Petaflops**,</span> it combines **heterogeneous clusters** and **massively parallel systems** to tackle interdisciplinary challenges. <span style="color: green;">Specifically,</span> its **Intel Xeon Phi processors** and **42,752 TB of memory** enable researchers to **split problems into chunks** and process them simultaneously. <span style="color: green;">Furthermore,</span> the center’s **liquid cooling system** reduces energy consumption, proving that **upgrading with better processors** can balance power and sustainability.  

**Creative Thinking: Advertising in the Tech World**  
<span style="color: green;">Now, here’s an innovative idea:</span> imagine **interactive holograms** in public spaces. Unlike traditional **electronic billboards**, these holograms could let users **click on icons** to explore products in 3D. <span style="color: green;">For example,</span> pointing a smartphone at a holographic car might reveal its engine specs or allow virtual test drives. <span style="color: blue;">This approach would not only engage users</span> but also **facilitate communication between the user and the system**, merging advertising with immersive technology.  

**Conclusion**  
<span style="color: green;">In summary,</span> computers have **evolved from slow, room-sized machines** to indispensable tools that **accelerate calculations** and **store vast data**. **Supercomputers** like those at SPbPU push boundaries, while creative advertising ideas like holograms promise futuristic engagement. <span style="color: green;">However,</span> as we embrace progress, we must ensure technology remains ethical and sustainable. <span style="color: green;">After all,</span> the true power of computers lies not in their speed but in how they serve humanity.
